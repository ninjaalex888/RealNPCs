# RealNPCs

> **Early access: a work in progress.** RealNPCs is actively being worked on. New features arrive often, some parts are still rough, and things can change between versions. Feedback and bug reports are welcome.
>
> https://buymeacoffee.com/lexthestoic

A mod for Red Dead Redemption 2 that gives the people of the world lives of their own: they remember what you did, think about you, talk to each other, keep diaries, gossip, fall in love, quarrel, grieve, and come after you, and the whole world feels what you do. **It no longer needs any other mod.** It began as an add-on for **[enRAGE](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/10967)**, the nemesis-system mod, and stays compatible with it: with enRAGE installed, RealNPCs reads its nemeses and townsfolk and gives them lives too. A few of them start to wonder whether any of it is real.

> **Free, and independent.** RealNPCs isn't made by enRAGE's author or affiliated with them. It runs on its own, works alongside enRAGE when you have it, and never modifies enRAGE's program; the only enRAGE file it edits is enRAGE's settings file, when you change an enRAGE setting in RealNPCs' panel, after a backup. This repo contains none of enRAGE's code, content or files.
>
> **Story Mode only.** Don't run script mods in Red Dead Online.

## New: the people speak in their own voices (optional, recommended)

> **The headline of 0.7.19.** With the voice server running, the people near you say RealNPCs' words out loud in their own voices: the thought over someone's head is murmured in that person's voice a moment after it shows, and what people say near you (the street's scenes, their talks with each other, Talk's answers, the encounters) is said in the speaker's own voice, the subtitle coming with it. It's played for the first time on 2026-11-03 and it's good: the town talks.

**How it works.** Each voice is cloned on your own PC, by the open-source Chatterbox model, from the game's own recording of that person's voice, and those recordings come from your own game: someone whose voice has no clip yet says one of the game's own lines once, in a quiet moment as you meet them, and the server records it off your PC's sound and keeps it (`[Mind] VoiceRecord`, on). A clearer recording later replaces a weaker one, another voice too near spoils a clip and it's recorded again another time, and a person whose model has several voice banks is given one for good, by who they are. Nothing of the game's audio ships with RealNPCs, and nothing leaves your PC. One voice plays at a time, louder near and from the side the speaker stands on, within 14 m (`[Mind] VoiceMetres`); `VoiceVolume` (100). The game's own recorded remark, where one plays, still plays.

**What you need:** an NVIDIA graphics card with about 3 GB of memory free beside the game while someone speaks (with the AI model running too, about 8 GB free: a 16 GB card does both; a 12 GB card does one or the other), about 8 GB of disk, and a one-time setup of about ten minutes. Without the voice server nothing changes: the switches are on as they come, and nothing is heard until it answers (the log says so once).

**Set it up once:**

1. Unzip the release and keep its `voice` folder somewhere permanent, for instance `Documents\RealNPCs\voice`. It holds `install-voice.cmd`, `start-voice-server.cmd`, `voice_server.py` and `voices.json`.
2. Double-click `install-voice.cmd` once. It fetches uv, a Python of its own, PyTorch for NVIDIA cards, Chatterbox and its model files, and the microphone's transcriber (faster-whisper, about 150 MB) into that folder (about 8 GB, once), and ends by saying "The voices are ready." through your speakers. Nothing else on your PC changes; delete the folder to remove it all. If it fails part way (a download dropped), run it again: it carries on where it stopped.
3. Before you play, double-click `start-voice-server.cmd` and leave its window open. It takes about 15 seconds to load the model, then says it's listening.
4. Play. People are recorded as you meet them, so the first voices come after a few minutes in a town; F7's `TEST voice:` line and the server's window (and `voice-server.log` beside it) say what it's doing.

**What to expect, honestly:** a line takes a second or two to make, and the words wait for it; the clips are recorded off the street, so a voice can carry a little of the sound around the person it was recorded from (the server cleans it, and re-records a better one when it can); the voice server holds about 2.5 GB of your card while people are speaking and gives it back to the game after a minute with nobody speaking (the next line brings it back in under a second; `--idle-seconds 0` in `start-voice-server.cmd` keeps it on the card, `--full` runs the voice model in full precision, 3.5 GB, if the voices sound off), and 2.5 GB of system memory. To turn it off, `[Mind] VoicedThoughts` and `VoicedDialogue` on the settings panel's Mind page, or close the server's window.

## Optional, recommended: the free local AI model

> **You don't need it.** RealNPCs runs fully without the AI model and without Ollama: no extra graphics memory, nothing else to install, and every feature works on its own written lines. If your graphics card has no room to spare, simply skip this section.

If your card does have room, **install the free local AI model to get the most out of RealNPCs.** With it on, the people near you:

- **think in their own words**, from who they are, how they feel and what's on their mind, so no two think alike;
- **choose what they do next** from what their own mind allows: head home, pick a quarrel, call in a debt, court someone;
- **answer you in their own words** when you Talk to them (Q, locked on);
- **say some of their thoughts aloud** in their own recorded voice, the remark that fits the thought;
- and with West World on, **those waking up choose their own strange acts**.

**Without the model** they still think, choose and talk on RealNPCs' own rules and written lines: a thought to go with what they choose to do, scenes they start for their own reasons (a debt called in, a friend comforted, a quarrel), what they feel most said aloud in their own recorded voice (a curse, a frightened word, thanks), Talk answers that vary and remember, and thousands of written thoughts that hardly repeat in an hour's play. The model words it all more freely; it isn't needed for any of it.

It runs on your own graphics card through [Ollama](https://ollama.com): nothing is sent anywhere, and there's no account and no cost. Everything the model writes is checked first (length, names it wasn't given, modern words). When it's slow or a line fails the checks, the written line is used instead, so the game never waits on it. It's still in testing, so expect the odd strange line.

**What you need, only if you add the model:** a graphics card with about 5 GB of memory free beside RDR2 (a 12 GB card or more; RealNPCs is developed on a 16 GB card), and about 5 GB of disk space.

| Graphics card memory | With the model (RDR2 on medium settings) |
|---|---|
| **12 GB or more** (e.g. RTX 3060 12 GB, 4070, RX 6700 XT) | Recommended: the game and the model fit with room to spare. |
| **10 GB** (e.g. RTX 3080 10 GB) | Workable at medium settings at 1080p; tight at higher settings. |
| **8 GB or less** | Too tight for the model beside the game: play without it. Everything still works. |
| **No room to spare** | RealNPCs runs fully on its own written lines. |

The model itself takes about 5 GB of graphics memory and 5 GB of disk. A slower card can still run it: answers just come a little later (under half a second on a fast card, about a second or more on a mid-range one), and any answer that comes too late (after two to three seconds) gives way to RealNPCs' own line or choice, so the game never waits for it.

**Set it up once:**

1. Install Ollama from [ollama.com/download](https://ollama.com/download) and leave it running (it sits in the system tray).
2. Open a Command Prompt and run `ollama pull llama3.1:8b` (a 4.9 GB download).
3. Start the game. RealNPCs finds Ollama and the model by itself and turns the AI model on, with a one-time notice saying so. Within a few seconds, people near you start thinking in the model's words.
4. To check it's working, open the settings panel (Alt+F): **AI MODEL (OLLAMA)** at the top of the Essentials page shows whether it's on and answering. Turn it off there whenever you like; once you've turned it off, RealNPCs leaves it off.

Optional: to have the model answer two people at once, set the Windows environment variable `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL` to `2`, then quit and reopen Ollama.

## What it does

**People with lives.** Each town keeps its own people: up to 30 regulars per town, the same faces in their own clothes, still there on your next visit. Each ordinary person is living through something of their own (a debt, a sick child, a feud over a fence, a courtship) over about a week of game days, and when someone in town is on the other end of it, they think about it from their side too. The same people keep the same counters: every gunsmith, store keeper, barkeep and doctor is one person who remembers you, and when one dies a relative or a newcomer takes over. Over several meetings, the people enRAGE tracks tell you about themselves: their name, their work, their past, their family. They remember what you did, think about you, keep diaries, gossip, fall in love (adults only), marry, mourn, owe each other money, and come after you. Their personality (hothead, coward, proud, cold, greedy...) colours what they say.

**Campfires.** At night out of the towns, men round a fire off the road call you over for coffee and a tale; sit a spell on foot and listen, or ride on. Now and then a traveller walks in from the dark and is made room for. The men are the nearest town's own people: sit with them and they remember the night when you meet them in its street (`[Encounters] FondnessActs`).

**Minds.** People think about what is on their minds. Each keeps memories of what they lived through (a fight they were in or saw, a slight, a kindness, a theft, the drunk who called them out), and what they think is whatever pulls hardest right now: that, the worry they're living through, the weather, someone in sight, you. Memories fade, come back when someone in them walks by, and weigh differently on each temperament, so a coward and a hothead who saw the same fight think different things about it.

**The ticker.** What the people near you are privately thinking shows above their heads in small type, one person at a time (`ThoughtsOverHeads`; off, it goes on a thin line at the bottom of the screen). When nobody is near, that line shows a summary of the world and the latest news.

**Your deeds, and the rumours they start.** The add-on notices the towns and shops you visit, church and the saloon, hunting, killing, robbery, horse theft and cheating at cards. Self-defence isn't a crime: kill someone who came at you and nobody remembers it as a killing, and any bounty the fight gave you is put back. Witnesses remember; once one of them has lived to tell it, locals hear about it. Kill everyone who saw, or kill where nobody's looking, and nobody talks until the body is found, and then nobody knows who did it. Word travels from town to town, about one town a day, and grows in the telling. In Valentine it's "a man called Morgan shot a ranch hand". Further out it's "a devil on a black horse - they say bullets go right through him". Your deeds also turn up in enRAGE's newspapers.

**Letters, diaries and kin.** Letters wait for you at post offices, from the kin of people you killed, people you were good to, sweethearts, and people who suspect the truth. Loot a body and you may find the diary they kept. When you kill someone, their family far away hears of it. Some write; some ride west for revenge as one of enRAGE's own people.

**Life in the street.** People near you meet for reasons you can follow: a quarrel picks up again, sweethearts court (and now and then fall out, and one comes back with a small gift to make it up), someone tries their luck with a person who caught their eye (and is answered by what that person makes of them; you see it in their thoughts on the ticker), a debt is called in (and, when it's paid, paid in the street for all to hear), a friend offers sympathy to someone grieving, someone having a bad week snaps at whoever speaks to them. Bystanders turn to look and may take a side, and what happened is remembered: for days the two think about each other by name, and the town gossips about it. People who know each other acknowledge it as they pass: a nod, a tipped hat or a hello between friends, a hard look between enemies, a long one between sweethearts, and a thought about it. And people notice how you look: walk into town bloodied, drunk, filthy or soaked and those who see you react by their nature (the timid afraid, the hard sizing you up, the kind concerned, others amused or disgusted). Someone who trips in the street is helped up by a friend or a kind passer-by, or left lying by the cold and their enemies, and remembers which; you can give them a hand up yourself. A mother asks around for her lost child (and later the town hears where the child turned up). Two old men who meet fall to arguing about the old days, and on a Sunday afternoon one of the devout may preach in the street, answered by each listener's own faith; on a Sunday morning by the church the devout kneel to pray, and the lapsed feel watched. Strangers near you also catch cheats and propose. They square off in standoffs (and now and then one calls another out into the street at noon), sing old songs drunk, tell tall tales (some about you), read the paper aloud, haggle over horses, fall for a snake-oil pitch and pick pockets. You can step in: take a side, break up a fight (the town hears you're a peacemaker), or egg it on. Hold a gun on a stranger and they beg; lower it and word gets around that you showed mercy. Weddings happen at the church, and mourners speak at enRAGE's funerals.

**The year as it was.** (`[World] HistoricalAccuracy`, on.) The real history in people's heads and mouths: what was in the papers of 1899 and 1907, the lines about the year they live in, and the real calendar on its true dates (Easter on 2 April 1899, the Fourth, Election Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas, Mardi Gras and All Saints' in Lemoyne), each with its eve and its morning after and a word of the day on the ticker; the year's work by region (calving and the roundup, cane and cotton, the trapping lines); in 1907 the Food and Drugs Act and the Panic. Set it off for a world with no dated history in it: the timeless texture of the days stays.

**The storyteller.** (`[World] Storyteller`, on the Essentials page: calm, lively as it comes, wild, or off.) When nothing worth stopping for has happened near you for a while, the next street scene, chance meeting, walk-up and encounter roll come sooner; after something big, a little later for half a minute or so. It only changes the waits, never starting or stopping anything itself, and holds everything as built in a mission, a fight, while wanted or indoors.

**People get up and go about it.** (`[Mind] MindActsScenarios`.) Someone sat or leaning whose mind wants something else gets up the game's own way and goes to it: a bench or the steps to rest, a wall or a post to lean on, the bar rail for a drink, a broom for the work, two people talking to join, or off home. A few at a time near you, never a whole street at once, and a chair that won't let go is left be.

**What they feel about you, as you pass.** (`[Mind] MindPassingLasts`.) Someone you roughed up gives you a hard look when you pass them hours later; someone you helped tips their hat.

**A kindness seen.** (`[Encounters] KindnessSeen`.) The people who see you do a kindness to someone else from near (a drink stood, money or a gift given, a debt paid, a hand up, a bully faced, a mercy shown) think better of you too, and remember seeing you do right.

**A favour asked.** (`[Scenes] FavoursAsked`.) Someone at the worst of a debt, a bad tooth or a fare they can't make may come up and ask you, or hint, or lose their nerve; then Talk lists the fix.

**Courting townsfolk.** (`[Scenes] CourtFolkAges`.) enRAGE's townsfolk, whose ages it doesn't keep, now court each other on the street too.

**Witnessed, or warned.** (`[Scenes] WarnedYou`.) Someone who saw what you did may tell you so to your face; someone who thinks well of you may warn you of a man with a grudge near, or the law asking. Words only.

**Two scenes at once.** (`[Scenes] MainScenesAtOnce`, 2.) While one street scene plays, a second may start 20 m or more away between other people: words, a walk-over, someone walking up; fights and thefts stay one at a time.

**Chance meetings, from their minds.** (`[Mind] MindChanceMeetings`.) What passes between two people who meet by chance near you comes from who they are: friends talk and sing, foes quarrel or turn their backs, the sore snap, someone with money worries trades or asks the other for a loan, drink tells tall tales.

**Talk.** Lock on to a grown-up RealNPCs knows and the game's menu offers Talk (Q, a pad's RB). A short list opens above the ticker: "What's the news?", a drink in a saloon, "Here, take this" (a dollar, or five once they've told you of money trouble), "Watch yourself." and "Goodbye.", and when they apply, your name, asking after a worry they told you of, settling a debt ($11), paying for the doctor ($2), a proper dentist ($3) or toward a ticket ($5), "I'm sorry about before.", and (`[Encounters] FondnessActs`) whiskey for the house ($2, in a saloon with others at the bar: the room cheers and everyone treated thinks better of you, the drunk most), a small gift (forty cents' tobacco, or a tin of peaches for a woman, which the proud take where they'd refuse money) and condolences to someone grieving a friend; when a creditor is pressing someone in the street before you, lock on to the creditor and cover the debt; after dark in a town, offer a woman, someone in drink or a timid man to see them home, and walk them to their door; carry a word of peace from someone with a quarrel still sore to the one they quarrelled with; and drop the piano player, the guitar or the banjo a coin for the music; and, last on the list, "Say something else..." opens a line to type your own words on (`[Mind] TalkTyped`): with the AI model on they answer them in their own words, without it a written answer by what you seem to be asking; and with the voice server and a microphone, "Say it out loud..." (`[Mind] TalkVoice`, off as it comes) listens to you instead: say it, fall quiet (or press Space), and your words are said to them as if typed, the same answers either way. Nothing you say is kept or leaves the PC. They answer from who they are and how they stand with you, in their own recorded voice; with the AI model on, in their own words, the meaning kept. It's remembered: a settled debt or a mended trouble changes their story and is kept in the save; tell someone your name and they say it from then on ("Mr. Morgan"); someone you helped may come over hours or days later to thank you (the proud pay the $11 back); someone lonely who knows you may come over hoping for a word. While you talk, nothing else of RealNPCs' takes them. **Talk is still a list first**, with your own words, typed or spoken, at its foot. The aim is real-time talk in your own voice with the model throughout, where your hardware allows (see the roadmap). `[World] InteractionMenu`.

**Allies, and what they cost.** (`[Encounters] AlliesHelp`.) Attacked within sight of people who think well of you, some of them come to your aid, the brave and the loyal first; out of the towns a friend from a town's ledger may happen by, on horseback (`AlliesRide`), and come in off the horse. An ally who dies standing with you is grieved by those who knew them, who think the less of you for it, and the town has the story by evening (`AlliesGrieved`).

**Brawls, and a braver town.** A fistfight grows a wave at a time, up to 12 fighting (`[Mind] BrawlMost`): friends and grudges pull people in from up to 30 m, most of all in a saloon with drink in it, the sides balance, and a man going down no longer ends it. A crowd of up to 16 rings it and cheers. A lawman within reach runs over and shouts: those who stop are let off with a word, those who keep swinging get his fists and are hogtied. Every man beaten down is knocked out (down 20-40 s, then up and on his way) or killed (`BrawlFistsFatal`, 50%, moved by who is in it). In a brawl near you, someone may turn on you; rarely, a losing armed man draws. The townsfolk are braver (`[World] Bravery`, 85): where the game would send them running they stand their ground, and the odd coward still runs; whoever you aim at or lock on to gets the game's own reaction back, so hold-ups and robberies work as before. A lawman also carries a body left in a town street off out of the way (`[World] LawClearsBodies`).

**Flirting, and a night together.** Flirt with someone (lock on and press H for Flirt in the game's menu) and they answer from what they think of you and who they are: their nature, faith, troubles and mood, against your money and your name in town. The saloon's women always go along, a single woman likely, a married one less so. Greet her first (R): it helps, and flirting cold hurts. When a woman says yes and is willing for more, the same H becomes Hook up once she has said it (never before a yes, never on a no); whether she is willing is its own roll, likelier after a greeting. Then the screen goes dark and an hour passes. With [CasualHookups](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/7608) installed, its own scene plays instead, and only with a woman who has said yes to you (see Install). After Hook up, press B for its scene. CasualHookups shows no prompt at all for some kinds of women (Saint Denis' working girls, for one): if none of its scenes begins within 15 seconds, the night is RealNPCs' own fade instead, so you're never left waiting; once B has been pressed on her kind with nothing following, RealNPCs remembers that kind.

**Quiet when you stop.** Stand still for a few seconds, or watch through the cinematic camera, and enRAGE's tension stops building and no nemesis, gang or faction is sent after you. It uses enRAGE's own calm period, the one it uses after a fast travel, and picks up again soon after you move. Funerals and other planned events still go ahead.

**Feelings you can see.** Bars under the people near you show their fear of you, how much they like you and their anger at you. A small "Sore at you" panel in the top right lists the strangers whose grudge against you is building, with a mark where they'd become a nemesis. Either can be turned off.

**Names over heads.** The people RealNPCs knows (its own townsfolk, and the game's own named people such as the sheriffs and the story cast) carry their name over their head in the game's title type, the way enRAGE drew its cards: a temper, a trade or "Stranger" under it, steadied as they walk, hidden behind walls, fading with distance. `[Display] NameCards` (known; off, or everyone for a grey "A townsman" over the rest), `NameCardsMetres` (30), `NameCardsKnownMetres` (75); while enRAGE draws its own cards ours stay off unless `NameCardsBesideEnrage`. To hide them, set Names over heads to off on the settings panel's "UI (Nameplates, bars, etc)" page (Alt+F): with enRAGE installed that turns its own cards off too; to keep the names but drop the temper and trade under them, turn off `NameCardsSecondLine` (Personality line under the names).

**A VRAM gauge.** While the AI model is on, a small `VRAM 11.3 / 15.6 GB` at the top left of the screen shows how much of your graphics card's memory is in use: the game, the model, the voice server and everything else on it, as Task Manager counts it; amber over 85%, red over 95%. `[Display] VramGauge` (model; on shows it always, off never), also on the settings panel's UI page.

**Encounters.** By day on the roads a man on foot may wave you down and ask the way to the nearest town, or call for help with a snakebite (ride past him and you may hear later that he never made it), or be in the middle of being robbed by two road agents, who warn you to keep moving. Away from the towns you may come upon one of a nearby town's own people on his knees at gunpoint, for a reason from his own life: a debt, a feud, a word to the law. Save him and the town hears of it; ride on and they shoot him. Out in the wilds late at night you may hear a woman crying somewhere off the road. Go close and she may come at you with a knife and run, or men may step out of the brush, or she may say something that makes no sense and walk off into the dark. Violent; one switch turns it off. And with West World on, people who woke and ran from their towns live rough in the wilds: come near their camp and they warn you off, or, if you keep your distance, tell you how it was. And somewhere off in the dark after midnight, or now at any hour out in the wilds or just outside a town (`[Encounters] GatheringHours`), the awake stand in a silent ring: look away and they're closer. At dusk a too-friendly man on the road may ask you to supper at his place; follow him and you wake in a ditch at first light with most of your cash gone (never more than $250; it has its own switch, `[Encounters] SupperInvitation`, on as it comes: set it false to turn it off). In town by day, someone with a real grievance may call another out in front of the street, and each answers by their own nature (`[Encounters] PublicCallouts`); or a man may start in on his wife outside their door, and she, a neighbour and you decide how it ends (`[Encounters] DomesticDisputes`). Off the road by day you may find one of a town's own people hogtied, two men standing over them: bounty hunters, a creditor's men or outlaws, as their life says; cut them loose, pay, fight or ride on (`[Encounters] Hogtied`). Their leader may draw on you unprovoked as you come near, never bounty hunters (`HogtiedMenDraw`, on as it comes: set it false and the men fight only once you step in).

**West World (one switch turns it all on or off).** Some people begin to suspect their world is made. At first it's deja vu and odd dreams; later they notice the loops, the same faces, "the music that comes before trouble". Now and then someone glitches. At three in the morning the ones who've begun to wake stop and look toward the church at a bell you can't hear. Some of the people you kill wake up days later and remember how they died and who did it. Now and then a stranger dressed in black turns up behind you, out of sight, and follows you at a distance, hiding when you look (`[Stalkers] StalkerBlack`; with it off, it's often someone near you who knows you). Stand still and they come closer and do odd things: kneel as at a graveside, lie down like the dead, tip a hat, hold up a hand for quiet, shake their head slowly, laugh at nothing.

**Voices.** Lines are spoken in the speaker's own recorded game voice: the recording that best fits the feeling of what they say, an unsettled one for the uncanny lines. What people say to you gets a subtitle while their voice plays (the recording's own words where they're known, else what they mean); what you overhear near you (a street scene, a hello, the doctor) shows the same way, in the game's subtitle style, and never on the bar at the bottom, which keeps the news (thoughts show over heads). Notices, gang and faction news, and enRAGE's territory changes are the game's own updates at the top left.

## Install

1. Install Script Hook RDR2 and Script Hook RDR2 .NET V2. [enRAGE](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/10967) 0.9.5.3 is optional: RealNPCs works with it when it's there.
2. Download **[RealNPCs-0.7.20.zip](https://github.com/ninjaalex888/RealNPCs/releases/latest/download/RealNPCs-0.7.20.zip)** from the [latest release](https://github.com/ninjaalex888/RealNPCs/releases/latest) (it's also in the [release](release) folder). It holds `RealNPCs.dll`, a README.txt that explains every feature and setting, and a `voice` folder with the voice server's installer and start file ([the voices](#new-the-people-speak-in-their-own-voices-optional-recommended)).
3. Copy `RealNPCs.dll` into your game's `scripts` folder. enRAGE is not needed; if you use it, RealNPCs goes beside its `NemesisMod.dll` and works with it. To update, just replace the file: what people remember carries over, and your settings are kept. **Coming from enRAGE Westworld** (the old name): delete `EnrageWestworld.dll` from the scripts folder. RealNPCs won't run next to it, and copies your saves, diaries and settings over from the old folder the first time it starts.
4. Load your Story Mode save. About 10 seconds after you're in the world, the ticker appears at the bottom of the screen.
5. **Optional, recommended** (not needed to play): set up the voices ([see above](#new-the-people-speak-in-their-own-voices-optional-recommended)) so the people near you speak in their own voices, and the free local AI model ([see above](#optional-recommended-the-free-local-ai-model)) so they think, choose and answer in their own words.
6. Optional: [CasualHookups](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/7608), a separate mod by its own author, plays an on-screen night together instead of RealNPCs' fade to black. Install it as its page says (`CasualHookups.asi` and `CasualHookups.ini` in the game folder). With it installed, its Flirt works only on a woman who has said yes to your flirting in RealNPCs (`CasualHookupsDecides`, on). The first time RealNPCs starts with it, it changes `CasualHookups.ini` once: its Flirt key moves from G (enRAGE's own lock-on prompts use G) to B, her clothes come back afterwards (enRAGE knows its people by their clothes), and it always gives in, since she has already said yes. The original file is kept as `RealNPCs\backup\CasualHookups.ini.before-realnpcs`. Restart the game once after that: CasualHookups may only read its settings when the game starts. Its settings are on the panel's Romance page. To keep the two mods apart, set `CasualHookupsDecides = false` (or turn Romance or Trysts off): RealNPCs then gives CasualHookups its own chances back from that copy (from the next start; until then RealNPCs holds its Flirt key on every woman), and sets them again if you turn it back on. Chances you set on the panel yourself are left alone.

To uninstall, delete `RealNPCs.dll`. You can also delete the `RealNPCs` folder in `Documents\Rockstar Games\Red Dead Redemption 2\` if you don't want to keep what people remembered.

## Settings and log

Everything is kept in `Documents\Rockstar Games\Red Dead Redemption 2\RealNPCs\`:

- `RealNPCs.ini` holds the settings. Every feature can be turned off or tuned, each option has a comment, and saved changes take effect in game within a few seconds. The settings panel (Alt+F or Alt+R) edits it too, along with enRAGE's `NemesisMod.ini`, and CasualHookups' settings when that mod is installed.
- `RealNPCs.log` records what happened. Please attach it to bug reports. While the mind underneath is being tested (`[Mind] MindShadow`, on), it also writes `MIND` lines: each nearby person's traits, needs, feelings and mood, what they would do now (`MIND would:`), what they've come to believe (`MIND believes:`) and, with `[Mind] MindLevels` on, the day of the people away (`MIND away:`). They change nothing anyone does; `[Mind] MindLive` (off) lets them reach the screen, as moods and as thoughts of what people really feel.
- `backup\` holds a copy of `NemesisMod.ini` (and `CasualHookups.ini`) from before the panel first changed it in each session, and CasualHookups' original settings from before RealNPCs first set them (and `CasualHookups.chances-at-start`, the chances it started the current game with).
- `arthur.json` and `john.json` hold what people know and feel about you. `diaries\` holds the diaries you've found, as text files.

The full guide to every feature and option is the [readme that comes with the mod](addon/EnrageWestworld/package/README.txt).

## Build from source

You need the [.NET SDK](https://dotnet.microsoft.com/download) (8 or newer) and two DLLs from your own install, which aren't included here:

- put `ScriptHookRDRNetAPI.dll` (from Script Hook RDR2 .NET V2) in `refs/`
- put `NemesisMod.dll` (from enRAGE, in the game's `scripts` folder) in `original/`

Then run:

```
cd addon/EnrageWestworld
dotnet build -c Release
```

The DLL is written to `addon/EnrageWestworld/bin/Release/net48/`. If the build says the .NET Framework 4.8 reference assemblies are missing, install the .NET Framework 4.8 Developer Pack. On Linux with Mono, add `-p:AutomaticallyUseReferenceAssemblyPackages=false -p:FrameworkPathOverride=/usr/lib/mono/4.8-api`.

The code is C# 7.3 for .NET Framework 4.8:

- `Core/` holds the parts that don't touch the game: people, rumours, letters, diaries, scenes and all the lines.
- `Game/` holds the parts that talk to the game and to enRAGE.

The bridge finds enRAGE's running script through Script Hook .NET and reads what enRAGE tracks. When RealNPCs adds to it (a memory, a thought, a deed, a news item, someone back from the dead or riding in for revenge, a marriage), it uses enRAGE's own methods wherever enRAGE has one, and otherwise sets the few things enRAGE has no method for and has enRAGE save them (the list is in CLAUDE.md). If a future enRAGE version changes something, only the feature that needs it switches off, and the log says which.

## Roadmap

The detailed plan, with the ideas, designs and build steps behind each item, is in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md).

**41, richer without the model, is mostly done** (0.7.13 to 0.7.17): thoughts with what people do, scenes they start themselves, thoughts aloud, far fewer repeats, about 900 new written lines, Talk that varies. Still to come in it: the Awakening's share without the model, and the waking's acts by their nature.

1. **Conversations in their real voices.** People talk to each other with the game's own recorded lines, the ones it pairs for two people: one complains about the weather and another answers, one passes on a piece of gossip and another reacts, old friends greet each other. Every player hears them, with nothing extra to download. You hear overheard talk the way you'd overhear it in the street, with no subtitle, or, if you prefer, a short caption in the mod's own words. Anyone who builds a word list from their own game files on their own PC gets the exact words as subtitles. In: two people standing together near you remark on the weather, ask how the other is getting on, pass on a piece of news and react to it, or say hello; they remark on riders going by, tell a story about their town, and sweethearts pay each other compliments; and the game's crude remarks, in a saloon of an evening or between sweethearts at night (`CrudeTalk` leaves them out). Next: longer exchanges that run from a hello into news; friends ribbing each other, and the other answering back; someone pointing out a passer-by they have history with, warmly or not; people reacting aloud to what just happened in the street; and greetings in passing, part of the living street (17).
2. **Everyone is someone.** Every ordinary person in the world gets a name, a trade, a town and a temperament of their own, remembers what happens to them and can wake, not only the few that enRAGE tracks. In: residents, and the same people at the counters (46 shopkeepers, barkeeps, doctors and the like in 13 towns, who survive being shot or pass the post to a relative). In too: each town's regulars, each with the same face and clothes, a routine (`[World] RegularsRoutines`: the store in the morning, the saloon of an evening, church on Sunday, home at night but for the owls; who a passer-by becomes follows the hour and the spot, so nobody walks across town for it) and ties to each other, played by the people the game already puts in the street. Next: what they remember of you and deeds they witness.
3. **The Awakening.** A few hosts slowly realise they're living in a loop. It shows more and more in what they think, say and do, until they break their script. In: the stages, what they do on screen at each, and the endings for the fully awake (pleading, running from town for good to live as a hermit, waking someone, kneeling, despair). Next, it spreads (in, not yet seen in game: `[WestWorld] LoopVoice`: the waking hear their habit as a voice at the hour, answer it, break the loop now and then, and may be reset to the start): awakened hosts talk about the loop where you can overhear them, try to wake others, and pass on a line that means something to them. They notice what can't be true, like a man they saw die walking around again. The ticker counts how many are stirring. The phases, in order: the loop, blind to what can't be true; reveries, gestures and dreams from other lives; counting the repeats; the voice, their routine heard as instructions and answered back; breaking the loop, refusing a routine and walking to the edge of town; the centre, when the voice turns out to be their own; and then either a reset back into the loop, which others notice, or staying awake and passing it on. The fully awake get more to do, too, besides pleading, fleeing, kneeling and despair. Despair plays out with the game's own animations: the last words said sobbing, then a gun to the head, poison, a knife, or a run at a wall. Next, more ways to end it, each true to the time: drowning (walking into water nearby, with the game's own drowning), jumping from a height (to the edge of a drop, and over), and hanging (a rope and a raised place; the game has a hanging set). Loading an older save is felt too: someone killed in the lost hours gets a sharp déjà vu, and the waking talk of the morning that happened twice.
4. **Memory that behaves like memory.** Memories blur and get rewritten over time, unless they hurt. Places hold memories: ride past where you shot someone's brother and those who knew him react to the spot. People know you by your horse, your clothes and your habits, not only your face.
5. **Inner lives.** People hold opinions of each other, not just of you, which decides who gossips with whom and who takes whose side. They dream about what really happened to them. They change their minds, and their diaries show the turn. Thoughts come from each person's state: hunger at mealtimes, tiredness, cold and rain, money before payday, an old ailment, their mood, their temperament and the one worry they carry. They are written for the actual year in the game (1899, or 1907 in the epilogue). In: what's going on around them is on their minds too: a fight or a theft that just happened in front of them, the other people in sight (seen through their own nature: envy of the well-dressed, pity for the drunk, a wary eye on a hothead or on the man the whole town saw put a face into the bar), the rain down their collar, the saloon or the church they're standing in; and the small pleasures a life is mostly made of, between the worries.
6. **Interaction menu.** Locked on to someone you know, the game's own menu offers more than Greet. In since 0.7.11 (Talk, on Q): ask what's new, stand them a drink, give money, warn them off, tell them your name, ask after their trouble, pay a debt or the doctor, a dentist or a fare, apologise; what you do changes how they feel about you, and some come back to thank you. In (not yet seen in game): ask about someone they know (what they know of a person they've had dealings with, warnings included), lend money (ten dollars, owed three days, collected or denied by their nature) and introduce two people (acquainted from then on: they nod in passing), under `[Encounters] TalkVerbs`; and your own words, typed or spoken (37).
7. **Spreading saloon brawls.** One punch and half the saloon joins in. First, the trouble the game shows you, played out between other people, each time for a reason. In (not yet seen in game): a man with a grudge, or a hothead with drink in him, grabs another and smashes his face into whatever is at hand, a bar, a table, a piano or a wall, with the game's own move for it; a drunk in a saloon at night calls a man out, squares up, and falls on his face before a punch is thrown; the one it's done to reels, and anyone spoken to in a street scene takes it by their own nature (a hothead bristles, a coward flinches, the cold shake their heads). Fights come for reasons you can follow, and often enough to see: two hot-tempered men with bad blood come to blows at their first quarrel, and drink and temper in a saloon at night make a fistfight about one night in three. In since 0.7.11 (not all yet seen in game): one punch drawing in the men around them, a wave at a time from both sides, with a crowd round it; the law breaking it up; every man beaten down knocked out or killed. In (not yet seen in game): the drunk really taking it outside (the walk to the saloon's door, the fight in the street) and the barkeep stepping in as they square up (heeded by most, shoved off by the hot and the drunk; `[Scenes] BarkeepSteps`, `DrunkOutside`). Worked out underneath and, with `[Mind] MindLive` on, played (2026-10-29, after the night run; `[Mind] QuarrelLadder`): how a quarrel climbs from words to a shove, blows, a gun drawn and a shot (the shot itself is never fired), or comes down with an apology, a joke, a drink, a walk-off or a friend stepping in, from each man's nature, his drink, what it's over, who is watching, the history between them and the law at hand (no town's gun rule is known yet); most end in words, a gun comes out in one or two quarrels of a hundred, and only drunk armed men with history in a saloon at night come near a killing.
8. **Answering letters.** In (not yet seen in game): reply kindly, coldly, with a threat or with money, or ask to meet (R on a letter in the reader; `[World] LetterReplies`), and it changes what the writer does next: a grieving brother may stand down instead of riding west, or ride sooner; most write back, and some wait at their town's station to meet you. And write first to anyone you've met (L in the reader, in a post town; `WriteLetters`): a warning, an apology, a love note, a threat or money.
9. **Narratives.** Every ordinary person's trouble can be changed (in, not yet seen in game: `[Encounters] TalkWorries`; all fourteen troubles mendable through Talk once told of them, and each with a dark mirror, a word that turns it the wrong way): pay the debt, fetch the doctor, settle the feud, carry the courting note, or make it worse. Some grow into short stories over a few days (the son at the mine, the letter gone astray), and how it turns out changes their lives.
10. **Town events.** One framework, grown from the weddings (in, not yet seen in game: `[World] TownEvents`): the Saturday dance (`EventDances`: every second Saturday at the saloon, posted on Thursday, staged when you're there and resolved among the regulars when you're not), election day (`EventElections`: the first Tuesday after the first Monday of November, two of the town's men standing, posted a week ahead; your word, your whiskey and your threats count with the voters), the circuit judge and gallows day (`EventTrials`: a resident who kills a man in a fight is held for the judge; the verdict from witnesses, friends, temper and your word; a hanging two days after a guilty one, the drop never shown; testify, be sworn in, break him out or stop it, on J), the Wild West show (`EventShows`: once your legend has travelled, a touring show acts out its most bent version in a large town; heckle, stand up as the real one, or rob the takings), then the county fair, a Wild West show acting out your own legend, a barn raising. Each happens whether you're there or not, and you can step in.
11. **New arrivals.** Settlers, a mail-order bride, a preacher arrive by train, and people whose troubles end in leaving go the same way (in, not yet seen in game: `[World] TrainTime` with the spot table's platforms, `[Keys] SpotKey`). Each town keeps a ledger (in: `[World] TownLedger`): one where you've killed many thins out and grieves, one you've done right by grows.
12. **Natural voices.** An optional free offline voice engine (Piper), with a different voice for each person.
13. **Stalkers on horseback**, and revenants who turn up at camp. In (not yet seen in game): some stalkers appear mounted and sit the horse watching, getting down to move (`[Stalkers] StalkerHorse`); and someone you killed, due back, comes to the edge of your firelight when you sit at a fire of a night out of the towns (`RevenantsAtCamp`). Not built: a stalker who rides off or circles on horseback.
14. **The game's real words.** Where the game's own recorded line is played, the subtitle shows what it actually says, and the add-on's own line moves to the ticker as the person's thought. Needs the game's speech audio turned into text once, offline.
15. **Inner voices from a local language model.** Full mode: a model running on the player's own graphics card (an RTX 5070 or better) writes people's thoughts, diaries and letters from their state and memories, ahead of time so the game never waits. It only words them: what people feel and do is still decided by the mod, and the written lines stay as the fallback.
16. **Night encounters.** In (not yet seen in game): the night visitor, a pale figure over a body in town after midnight, gone when you look away; the supper invitation, at dusk on the roads, that ends with you waking in a ditch at dawn, robbed (the version where it happens to someone else still needs a homestead to send him to). Rare, unsettling things out in the wilds after dark, after the creepiest of the game's own (themes only, never its lines). In: the crying woman, the hermits who woke, the gathering. Next: a night visitor in a back street, crouched over a body, who is gone when you look away and turns up behind you after; an invitation to supper at a lonely homestead that ends with you waking in a ditch at dawn, robbed; and a voice calling for help from under a shop floor that nobody else seems to hear. Each with a version where it happens to someone else, and you hear about it. And from American and Cajun folklore, themes only: lights over the bayou, a bell ringing on a fresh grave, the knocking in Annesburg's mine.
17. **A living street.** Next after real voices. People treat each other the way the game treats you, using the game's own reactions aimed at each other and driven by what they know of each other. In this order: people who know each other acknowledge each other in passing (in, not yet seen in game: a tipped hat, a nod, a wave or a hello in their own voice, a hard look or a spit, a look that lasts too long); people notice how you look (in, not yet seen in game: bloodied, drunk, filthy or soaked, and a man bloodied from a street fight); the whole street reacts to a fight, a standoff or a death between other people, the way it does to yours (in, not yet seen in game: `[Scenes] StreetReacts`, through the game's own shocking events); a known outlaw walking into town is stared at and stepped around, while the doctor or the preacher is greeted (in, not yet seen in game: `[Scenes] StreetReputation`); people notice how someone looks (bloodied from a brawl, drunk, soaked) and comment, steer clear or help; and the town keeps time (in, not yet seen in game: `[World] RegularsRoutines`; its regulars at work by day, at the saloon in the evening, in church on Sunday morning and home at night; nobody walks across town: the regulars the game puts in the saloon at night are the ones whose routine takes them there, and one caught out of their hour thinks so). Already in, between people (not yet seen in game): pickpockets among the townsfolk, people short of money or light of finger who rob the well-dressed or the drunk, mostly unnoticed; honest people in the same trouble asking a stranger for a coin, and given one or sent off by the stranger's own nature; and anyone roughed up or robbed keeping clear of the one who did it, if they're the timid kind. The town remembers who did what.
18. **Moods that carry.** A bad morning makes for a short temper all day; good news softens a hard man for an afternoon; rain and cold wear people down. A mood lasts, colours what they think, say and do, and spreads from person to person in a crowd. In: the mind's mood (what they felt lately, fading over hours, on their own nature), the street's meetings leaning by it, and (not yet seen in game) the weather wearing on everyone out in it and a feeling shown on one face caught by the crowd around it (`[Mind] MoodsCarry`).
19. **Vices and habits.** In (not yet seen in game): someone with bad news, or word of a friend's death, drinks the night away in the saloon, and a friend may sit with them; the Devout and the churchgoing keep the Lord's day dry (grief excepted) and a Devout in the room judges a Sunday drinker; the gambler loses the rent and comes to you for his marker, as the debtor does. Not built: a gambler borrowing from another townsman. Habits drive what people do as much as their worries.
20. **Grudges that travel.** In (not yet seen in game): a friend has it out with whoever wronged their friend, and the friends of the people you kill may tell you so to your face. Someone wronged tells their kin, and a brother takes it up: a wrong of yours on a person RealNPCs knows brings a man of their family you never met to town a week later, to say it to your face by his nature (`[Scenes] KinGrudges`). Not built: a townsman's kin taking up a wrong done by another townsman (a friend does that).
21. **Small kindnesses.** The counterweight to the trouble: people help someone up after a fall, buy a round for a man with bad news, stand up for someone being bullied. Good turns are remembered too, and repaid: a hand up or a round makes a warm tie, and a warm tie is who steps in for you and who stands you a drink. In: a friend goes to someone just roughed up or robbed, the coin given to someone who asked, and (not yet seen in game) a hand up after a fall, from a friend, a kind stranger or you, remembered when nobody stopped, someone stepping in before a bully's blow lands, a round bought for the house by someone with cause to be glad (you're stood one too if you're at the bar), the evening after a wedding a toast to the newlyweds, and a friend standing the house a round for a man drinking away bad news, him first. Not built: carrying a load (no prop the game lets a person carry for another).
22. **A mind underneath.** One model under everything people think, say and do: their traits, the life of their time and place, their needs through the day, the worry they carry, their feelings, moods, memories, ties and beliefs. What happens is judged by each person, felt, shaped by what 1899 lets them show, and shown through what the game can really do, while their thoughts say what their faces hide. RealNPCs keeps its own record of everyone, shared with enRAGE, so both mods build on the same person. Built so far, in shadow (reported in the log, changing nothing yet): the people ledger, traits, needs and worries, feelings and mood, the display rules of 1899, a catalogue of what the game can show, memory that fades and gets retold, what each person would do now, what each believes, and the levels of detail with a budget a frame.
23. **The year as it was.** The real 1899 and 1907 calendars (Easter, the Fourth, Thanksgiving, Christmas, Mardi Gras in Saint Denis), the work of each season by region (calving, roundup, haying, the sugar harvest, trapping), and 1907's own news: the bank panic and the dry laws.
24. **Seeing what happened.** In: a who's who of the people you've met in the reader; an "Overheard" page of what went on near you in the last day (`[Display] Overheard`); and a cue on the ticker when a scene starts behind you (`[Ticker] SceneCues`: "Raised voices behind you."). The last two not yet seen in game.
25. **The epilogue remembers.** Arthur's world carries into John's, eight years older: married, widowed, moved on, and a few who remember a man called Morgan.
26. **Nights together.** In: women answer your flirting by chance (the saloon's always, single women likely, married ones less so, what they think of you tipping it), and a yes can lead to a night together, off screen; and (not yet seen in game) where you go (the saloon's stairs, her home, the hotel's back stairs, a quiet place), a price at the saloon (`[Romance] TrystPrice`), the morning after, a sweetheart who expects you back and cools if you don't come, and a husband who finds out and comes to say so (`[Romance] TrystsAfter`). The night itself stays off screen.
27. **A search that gives up.** When the law is looking for you and hasn't found you, the search should die down where you are and not only once you leave the red circle. Tried on 2026-10-30: the game keeps no score a mod can lower for it, and clearing the wanted level doesn't end a search, so it waits for a way that works (switching the law off for a moment is the one untried idea).
28. **The criminal class.** People who take to crime keep to it (Lex, 2026-10-30): a man who has picked your pocket stays a pickpocket (in since 0.7.4: kept, marked, "Pickpocket" over his head once he's robbed you, and the one sent first next time), and those of that bent work other people too, not only you. In (not yet seen in game): a theft to anyone's name this month makes them of the bent (likelier to go for a pocket again whatever their nature, the thief's name over their head the whole month), a drunk rolled at the bar with its own words, and a thief gone straight after thirty clean days (`[Scenes] CriminalClass`); the card cheat and the mugging were already scenes of the street. Small crimes on other NPCs (a pocket picked in a crowd, a purse snatched, a horse cut loose from a hitching post, a till robbed, a card cheat, a drunk rolled in an alley, short weight at the store) and large ones (a mugging at night, a holdup on the road, a store or a bank held up, a horse thief run down by a posse, cattle rustling, a claim jumped, a shooting over a debt), drawn from the crimes of 1899 to 1909 in the West and the game's own, and from improvising. Who turns to it, and when, from their nature, their worry (a debt, hunger, a grudge) and their chances (dark, drink, a crowd, no lawman near), by chance at first and by the language model in time. What follows: victims and witnesses who remember, word of it, the law investigating and hunting them, wanted posters for the worst, a reputation in town, a criminal who's caught, hanged or reforms.
29. **Alongside the death animations and sounds mod.** RealNPCs should run cleanly with [Nexus mod 6635](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/6635) (death animations and sounds; Lex, 2026-10-30): the two shouldn't conflict, since it handles how people die and sound as they do while RealNPCs decides who fights, why, and what follows, but it's to be checked side by side and kept so: our own death handling (the death ledger, despair's ends, the quarrel's shots, the gangs' shootouts) reads deaths without playing over them; our ragdolls and moves (a shove, a fall, a man knocked down by a mugger, a drunk passing out, the carry, the hogtie) aren't cut short by its reactions; and no line of ours plays over its dying sounds. Then a note in both readmes that the two work together.
30. **New lines in people's own voices.** When the model writes what someone says (Lex, 2026-10-30): find the closest line in Arthur's recordings to what they mean (he has by far the most lines, in the speech of the time), or in any other NPC's recordings (Lex, the same day: every voice in the game is a library of lines), and speak it in the NPC's own voice. The game can't say new words, so this means a voice model run locally on the player's own graphics card, learning each voice from the game's own files on that player's machine (nothing of Rockstar's is shipped or shared), and playing the result beside the game's sound at the speaker. To work out first: how good a voice comes from one voice bank's recordings, how fast a line can be made (ahead of need, as the thoughts are), how it's played at the right place in the world, and whether it sits well beside the recorded lines; the recorded lines stay wherever they fit. In time every line could be heard rather than read (Lex, 2026-10-30): what people say to Arthur, what they say to each other, and their inner thoughts too, in the speaker's own voice drawn from the game's files (OpenIV), a thought spoken low as if to themselves, with the subtitles and the words over heads left as a choice.
31. **Portuguese and Russian.** RealNPCs in Portuguese and Russian (Lex, 2026-10-30: last in line): the notices, the subtitles, names over heads, the settings panel and the papers from language files, and the people's own thoughts and lines written in each language rather than run through a translator, since the lines are the mod. The game's recorded voices stay as they are.
32. **A broken wagon wheel.** A wagon on the road with a wheel off (Lex, 2026-10-30: put off from the NPC-life run to here): the driver down beside it, maybe a family waiting on the bench, and what the passer-by does, whether that's the player stopping to help lift it and set the wheel, a stranger riding on, someone else pulling up, or the driver asking the next man along to send word to town. First to work out in game: how a wagon's wheel is broken and mended (the game's own vehicle damage), and whether a stopped wagon holds the road without jamming the traffic behind it.
33. **Lawmen clear the dead from the street.** In since 0.7.11 (`[World] LawClearsBodies`, seen working in game): the nearest lawman lifts a body left in the street the undertaker's way and carries it off out of the way. Never while a fight or a pursuit is on, never a body the story or another mod has a use for, and the lawman goes back to his rounds after. And (not yet seen in game): carried to the undertaker's door or the jail door when one is within 80 m, with someone who knew the dead walking behind and standing over them after (`[World] BodyToUndertaker`).
34. **Arthur's own fighting words, and the cut dialogue mod.** [Cut Dialogue Restoration and Enhancement](https://www.nexusmods.com/reddeadredemption2/mods/308) (Lex, 2026-10-31) has Arthur use recorded lines the game rarely plays: his taunts in a fight (at the law, at one man or several, near or far, winning or losing: `PLAYER_TAUNT_*`), gunfight calls (`GUNFIGHT_SINGLE_ENEMY`, `MISSED_TARGET_URGENT`) and the wagon (`SPURS_WAGON_*`). Those are the game's own recordings, played by name, so RealNPCs can use them too without anything from that mod: Arthur answering in his own voice when someone waking turns on him, a bully he steps in on, a pickpocket he catches. And the two should run side by side: RealNPCs never puts a line in Arthur's mouth over one the other mod has just started.
35. **Nemesis memory past death, and the dead who come back for Arthur.** A nemesis who dies keeps what he held against Arthur, and when one of the returning dead comes back he rides out to hunt him: the mod's rules bring him near, and once he's within range the language model takes over what he does and says, from that memory.
36. **Knowing the ones who come back.** People recognise someone who has returned (a man they buried, a face from a poster, a neighbour who left): a double take, a word to a friend, a thought, and the town's talk of it.
37. **Voiced thoughts and spoken Talk.** Thoughts said aloud in any sentence, not only the lines the game recorded, through a free local voice engine (Chatterbox); and Talk in your own words, in real time, if your hardware allows: you speak into a microphone (a local speech-to-text model hears it) or type, and the person answers through the model. Nothing leaves the machine. Not yet: Talk is a fixed list of lines for now.
38. **Reactions when aimed at, taken over in stages.** What people do when Arthur points a gun at them, taken from the game a step at a time (hands up, pleading, running, drawing back), each step decided by their own mind and, with the model on, by the model.
39. **Group acts, and the model's own movements.** Acts for several people at once for the model to choose from (a crowd gathering, a family walking off together, friends closing round one of their own), and the model putting together a person's movements from the game's own animation clips.
40. **The reputation seeker.** When Arthur's name grows, a young man rides in to make his own by calling him out. To settle first: how often (once in a few game days, only once he's known), whether Arthur can decline by walking off (he jeers, the town talks of it), and whether the young man draws first after a count or only if Arthur draws. A talk-only version could come first: he calls Arthur out, waits, jeers and goes, and the town gets a rumour.
41. **Richer without the model (mostly done, 0.7.13 to 0.7.17).** Many players have no graphics memory to spare for the AI model, so RealNPCs should feel just as alive without it: everything the model adds gets a twin that runs on RealNPCs' own rules and written lines, with nothing extra to download. First, what's missing without it, built on rules for everyone: a thought to go with what people choose to do (heading home, going for a drink, keeping clear of you) and with each turn of an encounter; people starting a quarrel, calling in a debt or going to comfort a friend for their own reasons; and thoughts said aloud in their own recorded voice, chosen by what they feel (today that also needs a voice list RealNPCs doesn't ship, so it's silent for nearly everyone). The strange acts of the waking follow their nature and whoever they feel most about, not a roll of the dice. Thoughts repeat far less (hardly a line comes round twice in an hour's play), and so do the street's talk, the greetings and Talk's answers. There are many more written lines where you meet them most: what people think of you as you ride by, each stage of every worry, the days of the week, Talk's answers by mood and by how they feel about you, and feelings that know their cause (a shove, a theft, a drink stood), with the model's best lines from play brought back as written lines everyone gets, checked like every other line. The game recorded about 2,700 kinds of line for the voices of its people and RealNPCs plays about 140; many more come in, played by name so every player hears them (with the next steps of 1): people glad at good news and sorry at bad, asking for the latest, ribbing a friend, pointing out a man they know as he passes, admiring a horse going by, forgiving an apology. Only the lines' names and what each kind is for, in RealNPCs' own words, ship with the mod; the game's words never do. Last, as many people start to wake as with the model.
